Campers will act out and solve the ants-on-a-stick riddle (sourced from [Mudd math facts](https://math.hmc.edu/funfacts/ants-on-a-stick/))

> [!tip] Ants on a stick riddle
> > One hundred ants are dropped on a meter stick. Each ant is traveling either to the left or the right with constant speed 1 meter per minute. When two ants meet, they bounce off each other and reverse direction. When an ant reaches an end of the stick, it falls off. 
> > 
> > At some point all the ants will have fallen off. The time at which this happens will depend on the initial configuration of the ants.
> > 
> > Question: over ALL possible initial configurations, what is the longest amount of time that you would need to wait to guarantee that the stick has no more ants?

## Game

Setup two markers in an open space, about $6 \times \# \text{Campers}$ feet apart. These are the ends of our stick.
> [!rules] Bouncing ants
> 1. Campers arrange themselves, randomly spaced, in a line between the markers (henceforth, the stick).  Campers randomly face either direction along the stick.
> 2.  The Facilitator says "go" and starts a stopwatch
> 3. Campers run along the stick. Whenever two camper run into one another, they high-five and turn around.  Whenever a camper gets to the end of the stick, they step away and are free.
> 4. Once all the campers leave the stick, The Facilitator stops the stopwatch.

> [!facilitation] Guiding questions and extensions
> - Each camper counts how many high fives they recieved. Then everyone lines up, and steps forward by the number of high-fives. Who got the most?
> - (smaller groups) What the largest number of high fives any one person can accumulate? Setup and test this arrangement. 


Now play a similar game, where ants may phase through one another like a ghost.
> [!rules] Ghost ants
> 1. Campers arrange themselves, randomly spaced, in a line between the markers (henceforth, the stick).  Campers randomly face either direction along the stick.
> 2. Everyone takes one big step to the left. Now the people facing opposite directions are on different lines, and wont run into one another. 
> 3.  The Facilitator says "go" and starts a stopwatch
> 4. Campers run along the stick. Whenever two camper run past one another, they high-five and continue. Whenever a camper gets to the end of the stick, they step away and are free.
> 5. Once all the campers leave the stick, The Facilitator stops the stopwatch.

Campers should realize that, if you can't tell the ants apart, these two situations are identical. The time required for bouncing ants equals the time required for ghost ants, which is how long an ant takes to cross the stick!  Therefore, the answer to the original riddle is 1 minute.

> [!facilitation]
> - (5 minutes)  Present the riddle, and give campers some time to think it over.
> - (10 minutes) Play the bouncing ants game, and discuss
> - (5 minutes) Play the ghost ants game, and discuss. Compare times for ghost ants and bouncing ants.
> - (5 minutes) Discuss the core insight, and the solution to the riddle. Make the point that in math, a change of perspective can make a problem much easier.

> [!tip] Extension: the [two trains puzzle](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/TwoTrainsPuzzle.html)
>  > Two trains are on the same track a distance 100 km apart heading towards one another, each at a speed of 50 km/h. A fly starting out at the front of one train, flies towards the other at a speed of 75 km/h. Upon reaching the other train, the fly turns around and continues towards the first train. How many kilometers does the fly travel before getting squashed in the collision of the two trains?
>  
>  Split into groups of 3, and have the campers try to solve this riddle. Have two of the campers act as the trains, walking towards one another on the line, and the third camper as the fly running between them, high fiving at each end. Count how many steps the "fly" takes.
