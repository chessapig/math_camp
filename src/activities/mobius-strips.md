## Rules

> [!rules] How to play
> 1. Make a loop from a paper strip **with no twist**. Tape the ends together.
> 2. Make a second loop, but give one end **a half twist** before taping.
> 3. Draw a line down the middle of each loop without lifting your marker.
> 4. **Predict** what will happen when you cut along the line, then cut.
> 5. Record your prediction and the result before trying the next challenge.

> [!facilitation]
> - Pre-cut the strips. Kids burn a surprising amount of time cutting them neatly.
> - Insist on the written prediction. The surprise only works if they committed to a guess first.
> - Wide strips (1.5 in or more) are much easier for younger campers to cut down the middle.

## Setup

Put strips, tape, one pair of scissors, and markers on each table. Have a finished Möbius strip ready to hold up.

> [!figure] A Möbius strip has only one side and one edge.
> ![A loop of paper with a single half twist, drawn in blue](mobius.svg)
> Source: Drawn for Math Camp

> [!facilitation] Introducing the activity
> Ask: "How many sides does a piece of paper have?" Then trace a finger along the Möbius strip until you come back to where you started, on the "other" side.

## Challenges

1. Cut the Möbius strip down the middle. (One long loop with two half twists.)
2. Cut a new Möbius strip one third of the way from the edge. (Two linked loops!)
3. Make a loop with **two** half twists and cut it down the middle.

> [!facilitation]
> Campers who finish early can make a table: number of half twists vs. what the cut produces. Ask them to find a pattern for odd and even numbers of twists.

## Wrap-up

Gather everyone and ask pairs to share one surprising result. Introduce the word *topology*: the study of shapes that you're allowed to stretch but not tear.

> [!tip] Extension
> Show a picture of a Klein bottle: a surface you can't build in three dimensions without it passing through itself.
